World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026: US-mediated Russia–Ukraine talks — the first direct talks since February — ended in Abu Dhabi with a prisoner exchange, 1,000 or 1,500 per side mooted; Jared Kushner called it "the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners", but no ceasefire; territory remains the hardest issue (BBC). At COP31 in Antalya, 102 leaders opened the summit; UN chief António Guterres warned the climate crisis is "outrunning us", with the US away a second year (AFP). Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two tankers hit mines and burned in the Strait of Hormuz; 37 ships were attacked in October, the IMO counting 130 incidents since Feb (AP/Reuters). Ebola in DR Congo passed 11,042 cases and 5,387 deaths, its deadliest on record; Kenya discharged its last Ebola patient, starting a 42-day countdown to Ebola-free status (BBC). The House voted 211–204 to send impeachment articles against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, ending the push, and House Republicans elected Jim Jordan leader. British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer in England for suspected spying for Russia; the Justice Department indicted three Iranian-backed operatives over nearly 20 alleged plots, including one at RAF Fairford. Israel's new Knesset was sworn in with no government after a deadlocked election stripped Netanyahu of his majority. US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above the 0.3% forecast, putting a December Fed rise near-certain (Bureau of Labor Statistics). Anthropic filed to list on Nasdaq at a possible $1.4 trillion valuation; shareholders sued Nvidia and CEO Jensen Huang over a Chinese spying scandal. Ex-Malaysian PM Najib Razak left prison for house arrest after four years over 1MDB. At least 39 migrants died when a boat capsized off Senegal. The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed two Houthi drones and three cruise missiles aimed at Saudi Arabia; the UN warned over one million people in Lebanon face acute food insecurity (Arab News).
